Objective design · shortcut elimination · difficulty taxonomy · preliminary experiment

Every objective has a
cheapest cheat.

A model minimizes loss by the laziest route available. For behavior cloning, that route is memorization — a scene→trajectory lookup that needs no general understanding (the vision-over-language shortcut is one measured face of it). But cheats are objective-specific: a target that is not recoverable from the current input has none. Mix in enough shortcut-free objectives — hardest of all, the retrospective ones — and the cheapest solution that satisfies them all at once is no longer any single cheat, but the causal structure they share: a generalized representation. The axis below sorts every VLA objective by exactly that — how available its shortcut is.

Why mixing shortcut-free objectives works

The contribution of a hard objective is subtractive as much as additive: it deletes cheap solutions. The whole thesis is three steps — plus two conditions that are easy to get wrong.

1 · one objective → one cheat
Any single loss admits a lowest-complexity solution that scores well without the general representation. BC's is memorization — and the model takes it because it is cheaper.
2 · no common cheat
Cheats are objective-specific. Add targets whose shortcuts don't overlap — retrospective ones have none at all — and no single cheat satisfies the whole set. The cheapest shared solution is forced toward the causal structure they have in common.
3 · difficulty ≡ shortcut-freeness
"Harder" helps only in the sense that matters: shortcut-orthogonal to the rest. That is exactly the recoverability axis above — which is why the axis is the thesis.

Condition A — a shared, bounded representation

  • If every objective gets its own head/expert with slack capacity, the model memorizes each separately and nothing is forced.
  • The pressure toward generalization exists only when objectives compete for one backbone — so retrospective / world-model targets must train the shared representation, not an isolated expert.

Condition B — the shared solution must be reachable

  • Push difficulty past what optimization and capacity can reach, and the model underfits everything.
  • The target must be underdetermined-but-recoverable — the "hard-but-learnable" quadrant the experiment below is built to locate.
Why retrospective, and not merely hard

Shortcut-free is necessary, not sufficient — far-future prediction is shortcut-free too. Four arguments single out the past, each ruling out one alternative.

L1 · it happened rules out far future
underdetermined, but learnable
The far future is underdetermined and partly stochastic — much of it is noise. The past is underdetermined but it actually occurred and sits in the data: same difficulty, cleaner signal. ▸ predicts G_obs(retro) > G_obs(far-future).
L2 · the task lives there rules out foresight · introspection
recovering the past = encoding the task
The instruction is given at t=0; goal and progress are defined against the initial state. Recovering them from the present forces encoding task identity, phase, and the causal effect of actions so far — exactly what the forward cheat skips.
L3 · abduction ⊃ extraction rules out inverse dynamics
infer the absent cause, not interpolate
Both-frame inverse dynamics gives the endpoints → interpolation. Retrospection gives only the present → it must infer the absent cause, and know which pasts could not have led here. Inverse dynamics is its degenerate case.
L4 · cheat dies with vision on rules out LA4VLA
factorize invariant scene vs caused change
LA4VLA drops vision at the data level; the cheat re-enters when vision returns. "Predict the past" has no cheat even with full vision — it forces separating the static scene from action-caused change. Orthogonal, and composable with vision removal.

What the logic actually selects: not pixel-exact o0 (often leaves no trace → noise, failing L1), but the recoverable past — the instruction, what has changed since t=0, task identity and progress.

Two routes to kill the cheat

One disease — a policy that scores by memorizing rather than understanding — has two orthogonal cures. They compose.

LA4VLA — remove the shortcut's input 2606.27295

  • Data level. Pretrain on language-action alone, with vision stripped, so the policy cannot lean on scene cues. Mixed LA+VLA wins; robustness under visual perturbation improves.
  • Limit: the cheat can re-enter once vision returns downstream — it treats one shortcut (vision), not shortcuts in general.

Ours — remove the shortcut itself

  • Objective level. Keep every input, but add shortcut-free targets (retrospective) whose only solution is the general representation — with vision fully present.
  • Strictly more general: LA4VLA's robustness becomes a special case — removing an input is one way to deny a cheat; denying the cheat directly subsumes it.
Why the hindsight column is empty

Four families come close. Each fails the same test: the past is still in the model's input.

ReMem-VLA 2603.12942
loss literally named "Past Observation Prediction"
Closest prior art by name. But it is a recurrent memory model — history is carried in its input — and it reconstructs a recent frame ô(t−m), not the episode start.
Past-Token Prediction 2505.09561
predicts past action tokens
A temporal-consistency regularizer for long-context diffusion policies. It conditions on the observation history, so past observations remain inputs.
RONAR · DeChant 2411.12960 · 2306.09922
narrates what the robot did
The full past stream is fed in, and both run as separate explainability modules rather than a policy training objective.
HER · DIAL · HIGhER 1707.01495 · 2211.11736
infers goal / instruction after the fact
The completed trajectory is the input. These relabel data for training; they never predict from a current-only observation.
What the memorization cheat looks like

One cheap solution, several faces. Each is a documented way a forward-only policy scores high without a general representation — and each is measured in the evidence below.

Scene → trajectory recalldocumented
Given a familiar layout, the policy replays a memorized kinematic sequence instead of parsing the scene. The core face — a lookup from scene to stored trajectory.
Vision overrides languagedocumented
The policy reads the scene and treats the instruction as a weak modulator — grounding on the memorized training-task object rather than the words.
Positional / spatial biasdocumented
It grounds on where objects usually sit, not what they are; success collapses under viewpoint and layout shift. "Positional bias rather than genuine semantic understanding."

Preliminary experiment — target redundancy

The claim so far is logical: prospective targets carry a cheat, retrospective ones do not. This experiment tests it without training a policy at all, by measuring a property of the data — how available each target's shortcut is. It is the intended Figure 1.

Predicted placement — not yet measured. The experiment is a real test: if R1 lands next to P1 on the right, the taxonomy's premise fails. P1 vs R1 is the decisive pair — both use the identical trivial rule (copy ot) and the identical latent metric, so a large gap cannot be attributed to metric choice.

Two metrics, one plane

Measuring only difficulty is a trap: a target can be hard simply because it is noise. Pairing the two separates hard-and-learnable from unpredictable.

R_triv = 1 − Loss(trivial) / Loss(marginal)
G_obs  = 1 − Loss(probe o_t) / Loss(trivial)

Trivial rules no training

P1, R1 → copy ot. P2 → zero-order hold on the first action. I1 → dataset-mean progress. I2, R3 → per-scene majority.

That last one does double duty: if the instruction is predictable from scene identity alone, the vision shortcut is in the data, not just the model — measuring the cause behind the LIBERO-CF findings above.

Falsification gates honest

· R1 as redundant as P1 → episodes are too short (o0 ≈ ot); stratify by length, narrow the claim.

· Per-scene majority ≈ 100% for R3 → that dataset has one instruction per scene; the task is vacuous there. Becomes a dataset-selection criterion.

· G_obs ≈ 0 everywhere retrospective → the targets are noise, not signal; redesign toward easier targets.

Cost

Trivial baselines: CPU, hours. Frozen-encoder probes: one GPU, under a day. Runs in parallel with LAP codebase setup — and gates the rest of the project.
